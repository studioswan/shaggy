01/The Why

Here's the thing nobody says out loud.

Grooming day is one of the most stressful days in your dog's week. You drop them somewhere unfamiliar, hand them to someone they've never met, and drive away feeling a little guilty. For years the industry has built rooms that suit the salon and not the dog, and quietly decided that was fine. We didn't. So we started from the opposite end, with the dog in front of us and the person who loves them.

02/What Makes It Different

Your dog should have a person.

One groomer who's actually theirs, the same hands at every visit, who learns their name, their coat, and exactly how they feel about the dryer. The way you have a hairdresser you trust.

And then there's the cage.

We don't have one, and we never will. A dog who's already nervous shouldn't spend the day in a box waiting their turn, so here they don't. They stay with their person, start to finish, in a room with warm light and space to breathe instead of a fluorescent hum and a production line.

None of it is complicated.
It has simply, rarely, been done properly.

03/The Little Things

And then the small things, which turn out to be the whole point. Good coffee for you while you settle them in. A puppachino for your dog on the way out, every time. The same face at the door who's genuinely glad to see you both. Because it's worth elevating the everyday, and we couldn't think of a better place to start.

04/A Note From Joel

I spent years building grooming salons in the UK, and kept running into the same thing: a real craft treated like a hobby. So I built places that took it seriously instead, for the dogs, the people who love them, and the groomers themselves. They worked. Shaggy is me bringing that to Austin, and meaning every word of it. Your dog will be known here. That's the whole job.

— Joel
